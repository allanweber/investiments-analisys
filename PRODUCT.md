# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Self-directed Brazilian individual investor who actively monitors their own portfolio. Comfortable with financial terminology (CDI, IPCA, DY, preço médio). Opens the app to check positions, rebalance, add new investments, and track progress against personal targets. Not a finance professional, but treats their portfolio seriously: researches picks, scores investments systematically, reviews drift against targets. Uses the app on desktop and mobile, sometimes in a hurry, sometimes deep in analysis mode.

## Product Purpose

Financial Architect is a personal investment portfolio manager for the Brazilian market. It lets users score and rank investments using custom criteria, record and value holdings (renda variável and renda fixa), track allocation vs. user-defined targets, and receive contribution suggestions. The product exists to give active self-directed investors clarity: what do I own, how is it performing, and what should I do next.

## Brand Personality

Precise, Calm, Trustworthy. The product speaks with the confidence of a well-organized expert, not the cheerfulness of a consumer app. Information density is a feature, not a flaw. Every interaction should feel deliberate and reliable.

## Anti-references

- **XP / Rico / BTG apps**: navy and gold, promotional banners, product-push aesthetic. This product has no products to sell.
- **Robinhood / Nubank Invest**: friendly rounded shapes, pastel accents, gamified feel. Too casual for someone tracking a serious portfolio.
- **Bloomberg Terminal**: dense orange-on-black, data-wall intimidation. Precision without legibility is not the goal.
- **Generic fintech SaaS dashboards**: card grids, hero metrics, gradient text, blue accent on white, AI-generated sameness.

## Design Principles

1. **Information before decoration.** Every visual element serves comprehension. Decoration that doesn't aid reading or hierarchy is cut.
2. **Density is earned, not assumed.** Show what the user needs at each step; don't expose everything at once. Progressive disclosure over data walls.
3. **Consistency is trust.** Patterns repeat. A button that looks the same always behaves the same. Deviations are intentional and clearly signaled.
4. **Brazilian context, international craft.** Terms like CDI, IPCA, Selic, and aporte are used natively, not explained. The visual quality should feel like a well-funded independent product.
5. **The numbers are right, or nothing else matters.** Visual hierarchy always leads with the most important figure. Never hide or obscure a key metric for aesthetic reasons.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA. Keyboard-navigable forms and modals, sufficient contrast on all text and interactive elements, ARIA labels on icon-only controls. Dark/light theme toggle already present; both modes must meet AA.
